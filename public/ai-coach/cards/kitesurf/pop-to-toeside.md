# Card: Pop to toeside (פופ לטו-סייד) — DRAFT

> For coach review (Inbar). Our own words, distilled from IKSURFMAG (C&K: Pop to Toeside).

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| id | `kite.twintip.hooked.pop_to_toeside` |
| Level | Beginner → intermediate (first pop) |
| Prerequisites | [Switching to toeside](toeside.md) without thinking |
| What it is | Instead of sliding the board around to toeside, **pop** it off the water (like an ollie) and turn it in the air, landing on the toe edge. Often the first "pop" trick: the board leaves the water without the kite lifting you. |

## Stages, and what each looks like in a frame

| # | Stage | What should happen | What it looks like in a frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Approach | Riding on the edge with a bit of speed, kite **low: 11/1 or lower** (1–2 o'clock). Both hands on the bar, bar at the sweet spot (in, not pushed away). | Heelside, lines well forward (~45° or lower), spray. |
| 2 | Carve upwind | Turn the shoulders and twist the hips to carve hard upwind. Keep the weight back: bum over the back strap, front leg straight. | Board turning upwind, heavy spray from the tail, front leg straight. |
| 3 | Rise over the board | Let the shoulders come up over the board into a balanced, springy position, just before speed runs out. | Rider more upright, over the board, back leg loaded. |
| 4 | Pop | Stamp hard by explosively extending the back leg: the nose lifts, the board bites and springs out of the water. The **head and shoulders lead** the turn toward toeside. | Board leaving the water nose first; back leg extended; head and shoulders turned toward the direction of travel. |
| 5 | Turn in the air | Stay directly over the board, bar in close; push the original back leg through so the weight goes onto the new back foot. | Rider above the board, board turning beneath; bar in. |
| 6 | Land toeside | Land tail first with the hips over the back foot; bend the back leg, then drive onto the toes to set the toe edge. A small tug on the front hand brings power back. | Board on the water on the toe edge, rider's back to the kite, knees driving forward. |

## Common mistakes: what you see, and the fix

| Mistake | What you see in frames | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Carving too long and stopping | Rider stalls, board stops, before any pop. | Pop earlier, or carry a bit more speed. |
| Board slides instead of popping | Board skids round on the water; no air under it. | Front leg straighter, weight back, so the stamp bites. |
| Kite too high | Lines near vertical; rider sits under the kite; board slides. | Keep the kite at 11/1 or lower. |
| Pushing the bar away | Bar pushed out; power dumps; board sticks to the water. | Bar in at the sweet spot, both hands. |
| Sitting down with both legs bent | Board flattens, lines go slack, kite drifts up. | Stay tall over the board and spring off the back leg. |
| Falling over the nose on landing | Rider pitches forward on touchdown. | Get the hips over the back foot while in the air. |
| No power after landing | Lands toeside but stops. | Tug the front hand and carve back onto the toe edge. |

## Coaching decisions applied
- **Head always leads** ([coaching-decisions.md](../../coaching-decisions.md)): head and shoulders turn toward toeside at the pop; the board follows.

## For the AI: filming and frames needed
- A pop happens fast (~1 s in the air): sample densely (8+ fps) around the pop.
- Key checks: kite height (line angle) before the pop, weight position (front leg straight?), board actually leaving the water vs sliding, landing edge.
