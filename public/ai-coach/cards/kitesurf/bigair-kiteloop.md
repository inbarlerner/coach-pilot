# Card: Big air kiteloop (קייטלופ ביג אייר) — DRAFT

> For coach review (Inbar). Our own words, distilled from MACkite (Big Air Basics ep. 1: Learning and Perfecting Your Kiteloop), Shaun Bennett (Big Kiteloop Tips; Backroll Kiteloop Late Tail Grab), IKSURFMAG (Back Loop Kite Loop; Back Roll Kite Loop Grab) and Steven Akkersdijk (3 ways to land high jumps). Get High with Mike's loop videos and the Airush big-air mistakes list are still to pull.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| id | `kite.twintip.hooked.bigair_kiteloop` |
| Level | Advanced |
| Prerequisites | [First kite loop](first-kiteloop.md) both ways at 2–3 m · big sent [jumps](hooked-jump.md) with a controlled landing · a heli loop / [downloop](downloop-transition.md) to land |
| What it is | A high, powered jump where the **back hand** loops the kite a full 360° **on the way up**, about three-quarters of the way to the top. The loop swings the rider downwind under the kite, and the kite climbs back to catch them. |

**How it differs from the first kite loop card:** small first loops are pulled late, on the way down. In big air the sources agree the loop must start **before the apex**, while the lines are still tight. A loop pulled at or after the top leaves the kite no time to climb back, and the rider free-falls.

## Stages, and what each looks like in a frame

In big-air clips filmed from the beach, the rider is small and **the kite is usually in frame**. Use the kite's position relative to the rider: above, ahead (direction of travel) or behind.

| # | Stage | What should happen | What it looks like in a frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Approach + load | Fast, powered, crosswind or slightly upwind; kite at 11/1. Load the edge, ease the bar out a little to build power, repeat until the bar has plenty of pull when sheeted out. | Long heelside edge, steady spray; kite about 45° up, slightly ahead of the rider. |
| 2 | Send + edge | Steer the kite steadily up to **12, not past it**. Hold a long, hard edge, hips low, bar out while edging. | Kite climbing toward directly above; rider low, big spray from the tail. |
| 3 | Pop | Kite at 12 or just before: extend off the back leg and pull the bar in. Aim to go as **vertical** as possible. | Kite overhead at takeoff; rider leaves the water going up, not across. |
| 4 | Rise, compact | Bar in, knees up, body compact. Kite parked overhead. | Rider rising under the kite; kite stays above, not drifting behind. |
| 5 | Pull the loop (~¾ height) | While **still rising**, about 75–80% of the way up: back hand fully in, tight to the chicken loop; front hand pushes the bar away. **Commit**: a tight loop catches you sooner. Eyes follow the kite (centre strut). | Rider still going up; kite turning hard and diving in front/downwind; bar rotated steeply. |
| 6 | Swing under the kite | Keep the bar locked until the loop is complete. Legs in, lean into the pull, let it swing you downwind. | Rider pulled forward and downwind, body swinging under the kite; kite completing a **small** circle. |
| 7 | Catch | Ease the bar out **only when the kite points up** toward 12. The centre strut is an arrow: the kite flies where it points when you release. | Kite climbing back above the rider; rider's descent slowing. |
| 8 | Exit + land | From about 5–6 m up you'll hang under the kite: steer a heli loop (downloop) to land. Land downwind, tail first, soft knees, then carve back onto the edge. | Kite above or slightly ahead at touchdown; board pointing downwind; no big splash. |

## Common mistakes: what you see, and the fix

| Mistake | What you see in frames | Fix | Source agreement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Looping too late** (at or after the apex, often from fear) | Rider already falling when the kite starts to dive; kite still low or behind at touchdown; hard, splashy landing. | Pull at about ¾ height, while still rising. | MACkite, Bennett |
| Looping too early | Loop pulled just after takeoff; huge yank; rider dragged mostly sideways. | Wait until roughly ¾ of the way up. | Bennett, Airush (quoted) |
| Kite behind 12 when looping | Kite on the upwind side, behind the rider, at the start of the loop; low, rearward loop that doesn't lift. | Send to 12 or just before, not past; level the bar at the pop. | MACkite, IKSURFMAG |
| Hesitating / bar out mid-loop | Big, slow loop; kite shoots forward and close to the water. | Commit: back hand fully in until the kite points up. When unsure, pull harder. | Bennett, IKSURFMAG |
| Sheeting out with the kite pointing low | Bar released while the kite still points at the horizon or water; kite dives; crash. | Ease out only once the kite points up. | Bennett |
| Holding both arms tight | Kite turns little or stalls instead of looping. | Pull with the back hand and let the front arm extend. | IKSURFMAG |
| No exit at height | High rider descends with the kite overhead or behind and no second steer; drops hard. | From 5–6 m up, steer a heli loop to land. | MACkite, Steven (landings) |
| Legs left behind | Rider stretched out horizontally under the kite; board not under the body at touchdown. | Knees in, abs tight; drop the legs under you before landing. | IKSURFMAG, SA |
| Forward jump instead of vertical | Rider travels far across the frame before the loop. | Longer edge, steady send along the edge of the window. | SA (first-loop card), MACkite |

## ⚠️ For Inbar
1. **Loop timing rule:** our [first kite loop](first-kiteloop.md) card says "never before the apex, start late". Big-air sources say "before the apex, about ¾ of the way up". **Draft:** small learning loops late; big loops before the apex. Confirm, then add to [coaching-decisions.md](../../coaching-decisions.md).
2. **Middle heights are the risky zone** (roughly 3–6 m, per MACkite): high enough to free-fall after the loop, not high enough for the kite to swing you fully. Do we tell trainees to skip from small loops straight to properly sent ones?
3. **Head:** sources say eyes on the kite through the loop. That fits "head leads" (look toward the pull). OK?

## Coaching decisions applied
- **Head always leads** ([coaching-decisions.md](../../coaching-decisions.md)): the head turns toward the pull and follows the kite round; the body follows.

## Safety
- Build height in steps: perfect loops at 2–3 m, take care in the middle range, go big only with clean edge and pop.
- Steady wind, a kite you control well, never overpowered. Impact vest strongly recommended; helmet advised.
- At least 50–100 m clear downwind (the loop pulls you a long way), deep water, nobody in front.
- Practise both directions; for big attempts, land on your stronger side.
- After a crash, control the kite first; a kite left looping or diving can drag you.

## For the AI: filming and frames needed
- Full frame, rider **and kite**, from ~3 s before takeoff to ~3 s after landing. Densest (8–10 fps) from just before the apex to touchdown.
- Key checks: kite position at takeoff (overhead or behind?); is the rider **rising or falling** when the kite starts to dive?; loop size (how far the kite drops); is the kite pointing up when it stops; kite position at touchdown; board direction at touchdown.
- Airtime can be read from timestamps. Height can't be judged reliably: don't give metres.
- Far-away clips: bar, hands and head usually can't be seen. Say so instead of guessing.
