| What it is | A carve turn where the kite does a **downloop** (looped forward and down with the front hand) instead of crossing the top of the window. You exit with speed. It's also the safest way to get used to a looping kite's pull, and the first step toward kite loops. Useful when someone upwind blocks the normal kite path. |

## Stages, and what each looks like in a frame

| # | Stage | What should happen | What it looks like in a frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Approach | Good edge, kite at 11/1 (high enough to loop, low enough to edge). Bar trimmed so both hands reach it. Check the space downwind. Ease the bar out slightly to slow down. | Riding on an edge, lines ~30–45° forward, moderate speed. |
| 2 | Kite up, flatten | Both hands on the bar; drift the kite higher (toward 11:45/12:15 for first tries) and let it lift you over the board: **flatten the board** before carving. | Lines nearly vertical; rider upright over a flat board; less spray. |
| 3 | Carve | Weight over the tail. Toe → heel: sit back onto the heels. Heel → toe: push the back knee across, flex the ankles onto the toes. Look forward and downwind. | Board starting to turn downwind on its new edge; rider's weight back. |
| 4 | Pull the loop | With the board carving downwind (least resistance): a committed push-pull, **front hand pulls**, back hand pushes, **bar fully in**, and steer hard (≥45° of bar rotation) for a small, tight loop. | Bar rotated hard; lines swinging forward and down; rider low and back, board pointing downwind. |
| 5 | Lock in + follow | Keep the bar exactly there until the kite comes round. Don't dump power. Point the board where the lines point (downwind) and follow the pull; keep carving. | Board pointing toward the kite; rider compact, weight back; kite low in front, rising. |
| 6 | Level + finish | As the kite rises out of the loop: level the bar so the kite flies forward (not back up to 12), let the bar out to let the kite breathe, carve onto the upwind edge. Untwist the lines only when settled. | Kite climbing forward on the new side; rider back on an edge; lines twisted once. |

## Common mistakes: what you see, and the fix

| Mistake | What you see in frames | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Pulled violently / yanked over the board | Rider still edging or leaning back when the kite loops; launched forward. | Flatten and start the carve **before** looping; follow the pull downwind. |
| Looping too late | Rider already on the new edge before pulling the loop: edging against a looping kite. | Pull the loop while the board points downwind. |
| Bar let out during the loop | Bar pushed away mid-loop; big, slow loop with lots of pull. | Bar fully in, steer hard: a tight loop is a gentle loop. |
| Leaning back to resist | Rider leaning hard against the pull; accelerates. | Stand over the board and point it at the kite. |
| Arms twisted / lost the bar | Bar rotated past the push-pull position; kite crashes. | Lock in the push-pull position until the pull fades. |
| Kite flies back up to 12 | Kite completes the loop and climbs overhead; rider stops with no power. | Level the bar as the kite comes out so it flies forward. |

## Coaching decisions applied
- **Head always leads** ([coaching-decisions.md](../../coaching-decisions.md)): look where you want to go through the carve; once the loop is pulled, keep one eye on the kite and lead with the head into the new direction.

## Drills
- Slide-turn downloops first, then practise the carve alone, then combine.
- Learn in light to moderate power (15–25 kn), with lots of space downwind.

## For the AI: filming and frames needed
- About 4–6 s from the kite going up to riding away. Spread the frames evenly, densest around the loop.
- The kite loop often goes **out of frame**: infer it from the line direction and bar rotation, and say so.
