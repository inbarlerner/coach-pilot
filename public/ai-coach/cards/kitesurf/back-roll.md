| Prerequisites | Small hooked jump with a controlled landing, both directions · comfortable upwind riding |

## Stages, and what each looks like in a frame

The kite is usually **out of frame** in trainee videos. Read its position from the **line angle**: lines near vertical = kite around 12; lines leaning ~30° toward the direction of travel = kite at 11 or 1.

| # | Stage | What should happen | What it looks like in a frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Approach | Medium speed, riding upwind on a solid heelside edge, kite at 11/1, hands near the middle of the bar (less accidental steering). Aim for a ~1 m jump. | Spray off the heel rail; lines tilted ~30–35° forward; bar level at chest height; hips low, behind the feet; rider leaning against the kite, not hanging. |
| 2 | Quick send | A short, quick steer up toward 12, not further. | Bar briefly tilted (back hand pulled); lines moving toward vertical. |
| 3 | Level & ease out | **Before takeoff:** level the bar so the kite stops just before 12, and ease the bar out to keep the edge as the kite climbs. | Bar horizontal; arms longer (bar further from the chest); lines ~15° off vertical; board still edging. |
| 4 | Carve upwind | **The head starts the rotation:** look over the front shoulder as you carve, and the shoulders and hips follow. Hips low, shoulders level. **The harder the carve, the faster the spin.** | Head already turned over the front shoulder, ahead of the shoulders; board pointing more upwind than in stage 1, nose lifting; big spray from the tail; bar still out. |
| 5 | Pop, then pull | Keep the head turned; push off the back leg and extend hips, knees and ankles. Trunk and hands follow the head, with the bar level in front. Pull the bar in **only during or after** the pop. | Head turned further than the shoulders; body long and straight from feet to head, **no bend at the hip**; bar level, in front of the chest; board leaving the water from the tail. |
| 6 | Halfway | Body faces upwind, but the **head is already turned, looking for the landing**. Kite still ~12. Slow rotation is fine. Stopping here just means floating down backwards. | Body side-on facing upwind; head turned back over the shoulder, ahead of the body; bar level in front of the face; lines vertical; knees tucked; **chin level, not looking up**. |
| 7 | Spot & dive | Spot the landing as early as possible, hands following the head; steer the kite forward (toward the direction of travel) once the landing is spotted. | Head looking down toward the landing spot; body catching up with the head; bar tilting (front hand pulling); lines starting to lean forward again. |
| 8 | Landing | Drop the legs, board under the body, absorb with bent knees, ride away across or upwind. Lines are twisted once: untwist when stable. | Board under the hips, knees bent on touchdown; kite back toward 11/1; after landing, one twist visible above the bar. |

## Common mistakes: what you see, and the fix

| Mistake | What you see in frames | Fix | Source agreement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Over-rotation** (most common) | Rider spins past the landing point and lands sideways or crashes on the back. Usually with a very sharp carve (board swinging around) or lines past vertical at the peak. | Carve less; stop the kite before 12. To slow the spin in the air, extend the body (a long body spins slower, a tucked one faster). | All agree |
| Pulling the bar before the pop | Board leaves the water flat with no carve; rider goes straight up and barely rotates; or the board arcs around the rider. | Carve, pop, *then* pull. Rehearse the order on land. | All agree |
| Bent at the hips at takeoff | Rider folded at the waist at takeoff, legs and board swinging below. Low and uncontrolled. | Fully extend at takeoff: body in line with the lines. | Mike, SA |
| Over-sending the kite | Lines past vertical; rider pulled sideways/downwind; more height than planned. | Short, quick send, and level the bar early. | All agree |
| Bar not levelled | Bar tilted in the air; line angle changes from frame to frame. | Level the bar while still on the water. | IKSURFMAG, SA |
| **Head not leading** | At takeoff the head still faces the direction of travel, so the body doesn't turn: under-rotation, landing facing upwind or on the back. | Turn the head over the front shoulder as you carve and keep it turned; the body follows the head. | Coaching rule (head always leads) |
| Hands left behind | Head turned but arms trailing behind the body; bar behind the rider. Messy rotation, kite pulled off course. | Hands follow the head; keep the bar level, in front of you. | IKSURFMAG, SA |
| Curling up / leaning back before takeoff | Knees to chest and back rounded *before* leaving the water; hips sitting back. | Extend up and forward first; tuck only once airborne, if needed. | All agree |
| Looking up at the kite | Head tilted back, chin up. | Eyes on the horizon. | IKSURFMAG |
| Pulling the back hand for balance | Back end of the bar low while in the air; kite turns or loops. | If you need a hand, use the front hand. | Duotone |
| Hands at the ends of the bar | Hands wide on the bar; kite drifts with every body movement. | Hands toward the middle. | SA, IKSURFMAG |
| Too much power or speed | Big spray, rider hanging back on the lines, kite jerky. | Bigger kite in light, steady wind; medium speed. | All agree |

## Coaching decisions applied
- **Head always leads** (Inbar, 23.10.2026): the head starts every rotation and stays ahead of the body. IKSURFMAG teaches hands-first for beginners; we follow Steven and Mike instead. See [coaching-decisions.md](../../coaching-decisions.md).

## Key points (our wording)
1. Comfortable upwind approach, small jump, hands near the middle.
2. Quick send, kite stops just before 12.
3. Level bar, ease out, carve; a gentle carve gives a gentle spin.
4. **Head leads:** look over the front shoulder as you carve, and the body follows.
5. Pop and extend first, pull second.
6. Spot the landing early, steer the kite forward.

## Drills
- On land, no kite: walk forward while spinning, head leading, shoulders and hands following.
- Repeat small hooked jumps until the landing is reliable on both tacks.
- First attempts: bigger kite in light, steady wind, deep water and plenty of clear space downwind.
- **Film every attempt.** Riders are often sure they pulled the bar after the pop, and the video shows they pulled it before.

## Safety
Deep water, nobody within ~50 m downwind, no gusty or overpowered conditions, small jumps only (~1 m). Expect twisted lines after landing: untwist only once stable.

## For the AI: filming and frames needed
- Best view: side-on from the beach, rider ≥ ¼ of the frame height.
- Needed: from ~2 s before takeoff to ~2 s after landing. Sample densely (6–8 fps) around takeoff.
- Can't be judged from stills: exact send/pop timing, bar pressure. Say so instead of guessing.
- The rider may travel left or right in the video.

## Progression notes (separate cards later)
- **Send back roll:** kite goes past 12, so there's more hang time. Carve *less*, or you'll over-rotate.
- **Inverted back roll:** throw the head forward and down toward the toeside edge at takeoff; kite steering same as classic.
- **Big air back roll:** the spin comes from the pop (head and shoulders twisting under the front lines), not from the carve. The carve only stops sideways motion and sets up the pop. Learn it as a popped intermediate back roll without pulling the bar in first.
