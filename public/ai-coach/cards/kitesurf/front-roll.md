# Card: Front roll (פרונט רול) — DRAFT

> For coach review (Inbar). Our own words, distilled from IKSURFMAG (C&K: Front Roll; Hooked Popped Front Roll), Steven Akkersdijk (3 Ways to do a Frontroll; 6 beginner friendly tricks), Get High with Mike (steering when rotating).

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| id | `kite.twintip.hooked.front_roll` |
| Level | Beginner → intermediate (rotation) |
| This card covers | The **classic/supported front roll**: a small jump with the kite going up to about 12 (not sent far past), rotating forward. The sent, popped-low and inverted front rolls get their own cards. |
| Prerequisites | Controlled small jumps · ideally a [back roll](back-roll.md) |
| What it is | A flat 360° spin under the kite toward **downwind**: the back turns to the direction of travel, you look over the **back shoulder**, and land facing forward. Not a somersault: it's a spin around the lines. |

## Stages, and what each looks like in a frame

| # | Stage | What should happen | What it looks like in a frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Approach | Good upwind edge with some speed, kite at 11/1 (~45°), hands centred, bum low, back leg flexed but solid. | Heelside edge, lines ~45° forward, spray. |
| 2 | Send | Short, steady send toward 12 while easing the bar out to keep the edge. **Edge hard, but don't over-carve upwind**: too much carve makes a forward rotation hard. | Lines rising toward vertical; board edging (more sideways than pointing upwind); bar out. |
| 3 | Level + kick | Kite near 12: level the bar. **The head and shoulders turn first**, over the back shoulder; at the same time kick off the back leg **upwind, away from the board**, and pull the front knee up. Pull the bar in only *after* the kick. | Head turned over the back shoulder, ahead of the body; back leg extending; front knee rising; bar level; lines near vertical. |
| 4 | Knees up, rotate | Both knees up (compact = faster, more balanced). Keep the head turned and keep looking over the shoulder. Bar level, kite around 12. | Rider compact, back toward the direction of travel; head still turned; lines vertical. |
| 5 | Halfway | Facing upwind for a moment: don't pull a hand to "help" the spin, and don't look up at the kite. Keep turning the head toward the first sight of downwind water. | Rider facing upwind with the head clearly turned further than the body; bar level. |
| 6 | Spot + dive | Once the landing is in view: look at it, then steer the kite forward (front hand) for the landing. | Head forward toward the landing spot; bar tilting (front hand); lines leaning forward. |
| 7 | Landing | Legs down, board under the body, tail first, pointing slightly downwind; soft knees. Untwist the lines only once stable. | Board pointing downwind at touchdown; knees bent; one twist above the bar after landing. |

## Common mistakes: what you see, and the fix

| Mistake | What you see in frames | Fix | Source agreement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Throwing over the nose** | Rider dives forward over the front of the board at takeoff; kite overflies behind; board sticks to the water or comes off. | Kick upwind and away from the board: spin around the lines like a swivel chair. | IKSURFMAG |
| **Pulling the front hand too early** | Bar tilted hard during the rotation; kite dives; rider stuck halfway, lands on the back with the kite low. | Hands centred, bar level; steer only once the landing is spotted. | SA, IKSURFMAG, Mike |
| Head stops turning | Head comes back to centre halfway; rotation stalls; lands on the back. | Keep looking over the back shoulder until you see the landing. | All agree |
| Pulling the bar before the kick | Bar pulled in at takeoff; rider pulled up and over the board, no edge to kick from. | Level, kick, *then* pull. | IKSURFMAG |
| Carving too far upwind | Board pointing into the wind at takeoff; rider wants to back-rotate. | Edge hard, carve less. | SA |
| Kite never reaches ~12 | Lines still well forward at takeoff; rider pulled forward; under-rotation. | A decisive send to about 12. | IKSURFMAG |
| Over-rotation | Rider keeps spinning past the landing. | Stop looking over the shoulder once round; keep the bar in (letting it out keeps the spin going). | IKSURFMAG |
| Looking up at the kite | Head tilted back, chin up; off-axis, disorientated. | Eyes over the shoulder toward the water. | IKSURFMAG |

## Coaching decisions applied
- **Head always leads** ([coaching-decisions.md](../../coaching-decisions.md)): the head and shoulders start the rotation at the kick and stay ahead of the body until the landing is spotted.

## Drills
- Learn on a bigger, slower kite in lighter wind; deep water.
- Warm up with a few small jumps to rehearse the kite movement.
- Light fingers on the bar from start to finish; hands near the centre.
- Remember: if disorientated, doing nothing and floating down under the kite is safer than yanking a hand.

## For the AI: filming and frames needed
- From ~3 s before takeoff to ~2 s after landing; densest from the kick to the halfway point.
- Key checks: head direction at takeoff, kick direction (up/upwind vs over the nose), bar tilt during the rotation, board direction at touchdown.
- Mid-rotation, "left/right hand" swaps as the rider spins: describe steering as **front hand / back hand** (from the approach).

## Progression notes (separate cards)
- **Sent front roll:** kite parked past 12 (11:30/12:30), more hang time, so rotate more slowly; redirect just before landing.
- **Popped front roll:** kite stays lower; bear away, carve back, stamp hard; front knee up, head and shoulder dip toward it.
- **Inverted front roll:** throw the head back (look toward 12) at takeoff; stay compact; edge hard.
