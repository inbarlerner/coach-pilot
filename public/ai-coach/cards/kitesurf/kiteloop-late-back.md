# Card: Kiteloop late back roll (קייטלופ לייט בק) — DRAFT

> For coach review (Inbar). Our own words, distilled from Brad Strahm (MACkite: Late Back Heli Loop), Shaun Bennett (MACkite: Backroll Kiteloop Late Tail Grab), IKSURFMAG (Back Loop Kite Loop; Back Roll Kite Loop Grab) and the description of Get High with Mike's "How to Kiteloop Late Back Roll". **Mike's video itself is not transcribed yet**: it covers rotating fast, when the rotation starts and aiming the kite, so this card should be topped up from it.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| id | `kite.twintip.hooked.bigair_kiteloop_late_back` |
| Level | Advanced (big air) |
| Prerequisites | [Big air kiteloop](bigair-kiteloop.md), consistent · high (sent) [back rolls](back-roll.md) · a late back roll without a loop · comfortable rotating at height |
| What it is | A big-air kiteloop where the back roll **doesn't start at takeoff**. The rider takes off straight, rises, and starts the back roll **late, near the top**, looping the kite during the rotation. The loop pulls the rider round and the kite climbs back to catch them. |

**How it differs from a back roll kiteloop:** in a back roll kiteloop the rotation comes from the carve at takeoff. In the late back the takeoff is a straight jump, and the rotation starts high up, driven by the kite. Mike calls it "more scary than difficult".

## Stages, and what each looks like in a frame

| # | Stage | What should happen | What it looks like in a frame |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Approach + send | Same as a big sent jump: fast, powered, long edge; kite steered steadily to 12. **No upwind carve for rotation**, head facing forward. | Long edge and spray; kite climbing to overhead; rider not turning yet. |
| 2 | Straight pop | Kite at 12 or just before; pop, bar in and level. Go up, not across. | Rider leaves the water facing the direction of travel; kite overhead. |
| 3 | Rise, wait | Compact, bar in, kite parked overhead. Still no rotation. | Rider rising under the kite, square to the direction of travel. |
| 4 | Start the rotation, late | Near the apex, **while you still feel the kite lifting you**: steer the kite slightly forward. As tension builds on the front hand, the **head turns back over the shoulder** and the body (lead with the leg) starts the backside rotation. Hands near the middle of the bar. | Rider near the top, head and shoulders starting to turn upwind; kite just ahead of overhead. |
| 5 | Loop during the rotation | About **halfway round** (facing upwind, looking back the way you came), pull the loop hard with the back hand, back elbow toward the waist; let the front arm extend. Do it before the line tension runs out. Eyes follow the kite. | Rider side-on/facing upwind; kite turning hard and diving; bar rotated steeply. |
| 6 | Get pulled round | Keep the bar locked until the kite has looped and points up. Knees in, then bring the legs back under you. | Rider swinging downwind under the kite, rotation finishing; kite completing a small circle. |
| 7 | Catch + spot | Ease the bar out only when the kite points up. Spot the landing; stop pulling (a second loop is a different trick). If off balance, let go of the back hand. | Kite climbing above the rider; rider facing downwind again, looking at the water. |
| 8 | Land | Kite near 12 above you. If still high, steer a backhand heli loop for lift. Land downwind, tail first, soft knees, carve onto the edge. | Kite above or slightly ahead at touchdown; board pointing downwind. |

## Common mistakes: what you see, and the fix

| Mistake | What you see in frames | Fix | Source agreement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rotation started too late** (lines slack, already falling) | Rotation starts as the rider begins to drop; slow, wobbly spin; under-rotates and lands facing upwind or on the back. | Start while still rising, while the kite still lifts you. | Strahm, Bennett |
| Rotation started too early | Rotation begins straight off the water; kite loops in front; lots of sideways pull, little float; hard landing. | Take off straight; wait until near the top. | Strahm |
| Rotation from a carve at takeoff | Board carving hard upwind and body already turning at the pop. This is a back roll kiteloop, and adding the loop over-rotates you. | Straight takeoff, head forward; the rotation starts high. | IKSURFMAG |
| Loop pulled at the wrong point in the rotation | Too early: the loop doesn't carry the rider round. Too late: over-rotation. | Pull about halfway round, facing upwind. | IKSURFMAG |
| Bar slips out / one-handed and gentle | Wide, slow loop; kite low; rider pile-driven into the water. | Kite above you before you loop; steer hard, bar in. | IKSURFMAG, Bennett |
| Over-steering with hands wide | Kite jerks across the window; rotation and loop out of sync. | Hands near the middle; small steering inputs. | Strahm |
| No catch / no exit | Kite still low or behind as the rider nears the water. | Ease out only when the kite points up; heli loop if high. | Bennett, Strahm, MACkite |

## Coaching decisions applied
- **Head always leads** ([coaching-decisions.md](../../coaching-decisions.md)): the head starts the late rotation (back over the shoulder), then follows the kite through the loop.

## Safety
- Only with consistent big kiteloops and high back rolls. Most of the difficulty is fear: build up in steps.
- Steady wind, not overpowered; impact vest; helmet advised.
- At least 50–100 m clear downwind, deep water.
- Mid-rotation and off balance: let go of the back hand to stop the loop.

## For the AI: filming and frames needed
- Full frame with rider and kite, ~3 s before takeoff to ~3 s after landing. Dense (8–10 fps) from the apex to touchdown.
- Key checks: straight takeoff (no early carve)?; is the rider still rising when the rotation starts?; where in the rotation does the kite start to dive?; loop size; kite pointing up at release; kite and board at touchdown.
- From far away, rotation direction and head position are often unreadable. Use changes in which side the board is on, and say how sure you are.
