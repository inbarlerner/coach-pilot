## How to describe things (language rules for the AI)

- **Kite position:** say "11 / 12 / 1 o'clock", or "forward" (toward the direction of travel) and "back" (where the rider came from). Don't say left or right: those depend on which way the rider is facing.
- **Steering:** say "**front hand**" (pulls the kite forward) and "**back hand**" (pulls it back), named from the direction of travel on the approach. These stay correct even when the rider is upside down or facing backwards mid-trick.
- **Kite out of frame:** read the kite position from the **line angle**. Lines near vertical = kite around 12; lines leaning ~30–45° toward the direction of travel = kite at 11/1 or lower. Say so when you're inferring it.
- **Heelside / toeside:** heelside = riding on the heel edge, chest facing the kite. Toeside = riding on the toe edge, back to the kite.

## The default riding position (heelside)

| Body part | Correct | What it looks like in a frame |
|---|---|---|
| Kite | Around 45° (10–11 or 1–2 o'clock) for normal riding. Too high and you get lifted off your edge. | Lines leaning well forward (~40–45° from vertical). |
| Feet | Weight through the heels; toes lifting against the straps. | Board on its heel edge; spray from the heel rail. |
| Knees | Soft, flexing with the chop, pointing out over the feet. | Bent knees, not locked straight. |
| Legs | Push the board away, so the board sits between the rider and the kite. | Board further from the kite than the rider's hips. |
| Hips | Up and forward, close to the bar ("love the bar"), harness hook pointing at the kite. | Hips higher than the knees, not sitting down toward the water. |
| Shoulders | Leaning back away from the kite and twisted to face upwind, leading shoulder lower. | Body leaning against the lines; shoulders behind the hips (away from the kite). |
| Arms | Mostly straight, with the bar at the sweet spot (lines tight, kite not stalled). | Arms long, not hugging the bar to the chest. |
| Hands | Close together near the middle of the bar, knuckles over the top. Bar kept parallel to the kite's leading edge. | Hands near the centre line, not at the bar ends. |
| Head | Up, looking where you want to go. | Head up, eyes forward/upwind, not down at the board. |

## Common fundamentals faults (spot these in any trick)

| Fault | What you see in frames | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Sitting down ("toilet position") | Bum low near the water, hips behind and below the knees, board flat. | Hips up toward the bar, shoulders back, legs push the board away. |
| Alpine stance | Back knee tucked behind the front knee, front leg locked straight, board flattening and turning downwind. | Both knees soft and pointing out; weight through the back foot. |
| Pulling the bar in to hold on | Bar pulled to the chest or ribs; kite stalls or drifts deep downwind; rider pulled up over the board. | Hold the bar at the sweet spot; get support from the stance, not the bar. |
| Kite too high | Lines near vertical while just riding; rider bouncy and lifted off the edge. | Fly the kite lower, around 45°. |
| Hands wide at the ends of the bar | Hands far apart; kite drifting with body movements. | Hands toward the middle. |
| Bar trimmed too far away | Rider stretching forward with rounded shoulders to reach the bar. | Shorten the trim (depower strap) so the bar sits at arm's length. |

## Kite steering (the "front hand / back hand" rule)
- Know your front and back hand **on the approach**. Front hand = kite forward, back hand = kite back.
- When rotating or facing backwards, the left/right feeling reverses. Front/back doesn't.
- In the air: if you feel like you're **falling**, the kite is too far back → front hand. If you're **flying fast across**, the kite is far forward → back hand.
- Plan where the kite will be for each trick ("routine") instead of discovering it mid-air.
